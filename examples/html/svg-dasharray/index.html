stroke-dasharray

One attribute turns any stroke into a dashed one. It takes a list of lengths — ink, gap, ink, gap — and repeats it along the path.

1 · What "4 3" means

4 3 is two lengths, not two counts: 4 units of ink, then a 3-unit gap, then the pair repeats. Read it left to right as on, off, on, off…

The numbers are lengths, so the pattern is not "4 dashes" — it is four units of ink. Make them bigger and the dashes get longer, not more numerous:

A single number repeats itself, so 4 is shorthand for 4 4 — equal dashes and gaps.

2 · Guide lines & arrows

This is where you meet dashes most often in real work: a leader line pointing from a label to the thing it describes. Dash it so the eye reads it as annotation, not as part of the drawing.

The dash rhythm is doing the talking: 6 4 is a calm guide, 2 2 is a busy dotted rule. Keep the numbers small and even — an annotation should never out-shout the drawing.

Notice the arrowheads are separate <marker> shapes, so they are solid even though the line they sit on is dashed.

3 · Patterns you will actually use

stroke-linecap="round" grows each dash by half the stroke width at both ends — pair it with a short dash and you get round beads instead of ticks.

4 · An odd number of values repeats

Give an odd count of lengths and the list is used twice, so 4 3 1 behaves exactly like 4 3 1 4 3 1. That is how the dash-dot above works.

Same line, drawn twice — the red one just spells the repeat out.

5 · Marching ants

Animate stroke-dashoffset and the dashes crawl along the path. Move it by exactly one full period — here 8 + 6 = 14 — and the loop is seamless.

stroke-dashoffset: -14 in one @keyframes block. Used for "selected" outlines, loading paths and drawing-on a line.
