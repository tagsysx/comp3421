Complex paths — the d attribute

A <path> draws anything: a straight line, a curve, an arc, a whole logo. All of it lives in one attribute, d, written as a sequence of single-letter commands and their coordinates.

1 · The command alphabet

Each command takes a fixed number of numbers after it. An uppercase command uses absolute coordinates; its lowercase twin is relative to where the pen currently is.

CommandMeaningNumbers it takesExample
M m Move to — lift the pen and start here x y M16 29
L l Line to — a straight segment x y L50 10
H h Horizontal line — y never changes x H90
V v Vertical line — x never changes y V90
C c Cubic Bézier — the curve everyone uses x1 y1 x2 y2 x y C2 4 6 2 9 2
S s Smooth cubic — first control point is mirrored x2 y2 x y S30 4 30 9
Q q Quadratic Bézier — one control point x1 y1 x y Q50 10 90 60
T t Smooth quadratic — control point is mirrored x y T90 60
A a Elliptical arc — an arc of an ellipse rx ry rot large sweep x y A40 40 0 0 1 90 60
Z z Close — join back to the start point — Z

Numbers are separated by spaces or commas — M16 29 and M16,29 mean the same thing. Extra numbers just repeat the last command, so L10 10 20 20 30 5 is three line segments.

2 · Lines vs curves — and where the control points sit

A Bézier curve does not pass through its control points. It is pulled toward them, the way a magnet pulls a wire. The hollow dots below are control points; the dashed lines just show the pull.

Notice what each command needs: L and H/V take one destination, Q takes one control point, C takes two. S and T are shortcuts — they reuse a mirrored control point so two curves join smoothly instead of forming a corner.

3 · One complex path, built up in stages

Here is a heart — nothing but seven cubic Bézier segments and a close. Watch it grow: the same d, cut short at each stage.

The whole shape is this one attribute — the comments are not part of it:

d="M16 29 <!-- start at the bottom point --> C 8 20 2 14 2 9 <!-- up the left side --> C 2 4 6 2 9 2 <!-- round the top-left lobe --> C12 2 14.5 4 16 6.5 <!-- down into the middle dip --> C17.5 4 20 2 23 2 <!-- back up to the top-right lobe --> C26 2 30 4 30 9 <!-- round the right side --> C30 14 24 20 16 29 <!-- and down to where we started --> Z <!-- Z closes the outline -->"

Read the first curve as: from (16, 29), pull toward (8, 20) and (2, 14), and land on (2, 9). Every segment follows the same recipe, which is why a shape this curved fits in one line of numbers.

4 · The finished shape

Now change the numbers and see which curve answers:

Change thisWhat happens
M16 29 → M16 35The bottom point drops — the heart gets pointier.
16 6.5 → 16 3The middle dip rises almost to the lobes — more of a bat than a heart.
C → cEvery coordinate becomes relative, so the shape collapses: the numbers are now offsets.
Add transform="rotate(180 16 16)"An upside-down heart — no coordinate changed.
fill="#D9534F" → fill="none"Just the outline — you can count the seven segments.

One last thing worth seeing: swap the fill for a stroke and add stroke-dasharray, then animate stroke-dashoffset — the heart draws itself on. That is the same trick from the svg-dasharray and svg-animation examples, applied to a real path.
