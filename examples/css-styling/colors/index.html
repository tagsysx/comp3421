Colours — four ways to write them

Named colour — color: red;

Hex — color: #185fa5;

RGB — color: rgb(30, 79, 168);

RGBA — color: rgba(30, 79, 168, 0.5); (semi-transparent)

HSL — color: hsl(218, 70%, 39%);

background-color: #ffc107; — the colour behind the text

background-color: rgba(30, 79, 168, 0.5); — a semi-transparent background

hsl() — the third number is lightness

Lightness runs from black (0%) through the pure colour (50%) to white (100%). That is what makes hsl() the one to reach for when you are building a palette: move one number and the hue and saturation stay exactly where they were.

hsl(219, 70%, 0%) hsl(219, 70%, 20%) hsl(219, 70%, 39%) hsl(219, 70%, 50%) hsl(219, 70%, 70%) hsl(219, 70%, 100%)

Read the ends: 0% is black and 100% is white. So lightness can reach both ends of the scale, and the pure colour sits in the middle at 50%.

hwb() — white and black as two separate dials

hwb() takes a hue, then how much white to mix in, then how much black. Keeping the two apart is what makes it easy to reason about: adding white makes a tint, adding black makes a shade, and adding both together just greys the colour out.

hwb(219 0% 0%) +15% white +30% white +45% white

hwb(219 0% 0%) +15% black +30% black +45% black

There is no hsv() in CSS. HSV is what a colour picker shows you — the square is saturation across and value up — but a stylesheet only understands hsl(), hwb(), rgb() and the rest. Convert before you paste, and check the third number: in hsl() it is lightness, not value.
