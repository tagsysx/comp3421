3 · Choose the colours four structures · one named scheme each

Four structures, each naming the scheme it was built with. Give each one at least three colours of that scheme, then write the scheme's name and the hues you chose in a comment beside it. Every word has to stay readable on whatever ends up behind it.

3a · Navigation bar

scheme: monochromatic

One hue, three different weights of it. When everything comes from the same hue nothing can clash — which leaves lightness to do all the work, both for separating the parts and for keeping the text readable.

3b · Button pair

scheme: complementary

Two hues as far apart as the wheel allows: the safe action keeps the base hue, the dangerous one takes the hue directly opposite. Maximum contrast, placed exactly where you want the eye to stop.

3c · Info card

scheme: analogous

Three neighbouring hues, a step either side of the base. The set reads as one family, which is what makes it feel calm rather than loud — and it is also why you have to be deliberate about which part dominates.

triadic

3d · Mini chart

scheme: triadic

Three hues evenly spaced around the wheel, one per bar. Bold but balanced — no hue wins on symmetry, which is exactly why a triadic set needs one of the three to dominate the other two.

5 · Your own (homework)

The open task, with one worked example to start from: hover it and the ring fills from nothing to a full circle, and only then does the tick draw itself. Both beats are a dash pattern revealed by moving its offset — the same trick as exercises 1 and 2.

6 · Choose your colours (homework) three more structures, three more schemes

Same idea, three more structures — and this time nothing at all is worked out for you. Each card names its scheme; the colours, the placement and the comment are yours.

split-complementary
new urgent done mine

6a · Tag row

scheme: split-complementary

One base hue plus the two hues either side of its opposite — the softer cousin of a complementary pair. There are four tags and three scheme hues, so the fourth tag has to borrow from somewhere: decide whether that helps the row or spoils it.

tetradic

6b · Four tiles

scheme: tetradic

A square drawn on the wheel — four hues, evenly spaced. Four is the hard one: no hue dominates, so the eye has nowhere to rest unless you decide in advance which tile is the important one.

6c · Weather widget

scheme: warm & cool

Here it is temperature, not distance on the wheel, that does the work: a cool field settles back and a warm mark comes forward. Build it, look at it, then swap the two and notice how much more agitated the card becomes.
