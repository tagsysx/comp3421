mix-blend-mode

How an element's own content mixes with whatever is painted behind it. Every square below is the same #185fa5, over the same #ffc107 — only the mode changes, and the colour under each one is the result.

The arithmetic

Every mode works one channel at a time. a is the layer behind, b the layer on top, both 0 to 255:

  • normal — b — the top layer simply wins
  • multiply — a * b / 255
  • screen — 255 - (255-a)*(255-b)/255
  • darken — min(a, b)
  • lighten — max(a, b)

Check one channel: the greens above are 193 (behind) and 95 (on top). Multiply gives 193 × 95 ÷ 255 = 71.9, which rounds to 72 — and 72 is 0x48, the green in #184805. Screen gives 255 − 62 × 160 ÷ 255 = 216 = 0xD8, the green in #FFD8A7.

Nothing here was picked by hand: multiply keeps both colours and goes dark, screen is its opposite, darken and lighten keep one channel at a time. A blend mode needs something behind it — put one on a box with a transparent backdrop and nothing happens.
