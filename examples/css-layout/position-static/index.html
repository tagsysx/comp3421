position: static — ten boxes

Not one box on this page has position set, so every one of them is static. The browser places them itself — in the order they appear in the HTML, left to right along the line, and onto a new line when the next box is too wide for what is left. Ten borders and ten different widths make it visible.

2 · width: 310px
3 · width: 47%
4 · width: 74%

5 · a box with three other boxes inside it

Look at the lines. Box 2 (310px) and box 3 (47%) fit together, so they share one — and box 4 (74%) is too wide for what is left, so it starts a new line. Box 6 is only 160px, but box 7 at 86% will not fit beside it, so it too waits for a line of its own. Whatever is left at the end of a line stays empty.

Box 3 also carries top: 24px; left: 24px;. Find it: it is butted right up against box 2, no 24px shove to the right and no 24px drop. The browser read the two declarations and did nothing with them — that is what static does with the offsets.

Box 5 holds three boxes, and they are placed by the very same rule inside it: 5a and 5b share the line, 5c is too wide for the remainder and wraps. Box 5 also grew taller to hold them, because nobody gave it a height.
