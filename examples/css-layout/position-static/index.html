position: static — ten boxes

Not one box on this page has position set, so every one of them is static. The browser places them itself — in the order they appear in the HTML, top to bottom, each one starting a new line. Give ten boxes a border and a different width, and you can see exactly where it put them.

1 · width: 100%
2 · width: 310px
3 · width: 47%
4 · width: 74%

5 · a box with three other boxes inside it

5a · width: 62%
5b · width: 38%
5c · width: 80%
6 · width: 160px
7 · width: 86%
8 · width: 240px
9 · width: 58%
10 · width: 69%

Box 3 also carries top: 24px; left: 24px;. Look at where it ended up: on its own line, in order, its left edge level with all nine others. The browser read the two declarations and did nothing with them — that is what static does with the offsets.

Box 5 holds three boxes. They are placed by the same rule, inside box 5: in order, one per line — and box 5 grew taller to hold them, because nobody gave it a height.

No two of the ten share a line, however narrow they are: box 6 is only 160px wide and box 7 still starts on a new line. That is normal flow, and normal flow is what static means.
